How to Pay With Bitcoin or USDC

New to crypto? Start here — what you need, how to get coins, and how to send payment the right way.

What you need

  • An exchange account (Kraken, Coinbase, Newton, Shakepay, etc.) or a self-custody wallet.
  • Enough to cover the order plus the network fee — fees are small but real.

Which coins we accept

  • Bitcoin (BTC) — sent on the Bitcoin network.
  • USDC on Ethereum — an ERC-20 stablecoin, pegged to the US dollar.
  • USDC on Solana — same dollar peg, much lower fees, fast confirmations.

How paying works here

  • Place your order — nothing is owed yet.
  • We confirm the order and send you a payment address for the coin you chose.
  • Send the exact amount, then give us the transaction ID (see the TXID guide).
⚠️ Always double-check the address and the network before sending. USDC sent on the wrong network (Ethereum vs Solana) can be unrecoverable. Copy-paste addresses — never type them by hand.

How to Find Your Transaction ID (TXID) and Confirm Your Payment

We need a transaction ID on every order. Here's the easy, step-by-step way to find yours and paste it in.

What a TXID is

Every crypto transaction gets a unique ID (a long string of letters and numbers). It's your proof of payment — it lets us find your transaction on the blockchain and match it to your order.

Where to find it

  • Exchanges (Kraken, Coinbase, Newton…): open your history / activity → tap the withdrawal you just made → copy the "TXID" or "hash".
  • Wallets: open the sent transaction → copy the transaction hash.

Confirmations

  • Bitcoin: usually confirmed within 10–30 minutes.
  • USDC on Solana: typically under a minute.
  • USDC on Ethereum: usually 1–5 minutes.

Paste the TXID into your order notes (or send it to support). Once we see it confirmed, your order moves to packing.

Bacteriostatic Water for Research Reconstitution

What bac water is, how it differs from sterile water, and how it's used to reconstitute lyophilized research samples.

What it is

Bacteriostatic water is sterile water with a small amount of benzyl alcohol (typically 0.9%). The alcohol suppresses bacterial growth, which is why it's the standard choice for multi-use reconstitution in research settings.

Bac water vs sterile water

  • Bacteriostatic water: for multi-draw use over days — the preservative keeps it usable.
  • Sterile water: no preservative — generally used for single-use preparations only.

Reconstitution practice (lab basics)

  • Let the vial reach room temperature before adding diluent.
  • Direct diluent against the vial wall, not onto the powder cake.
  • Gently swirl — never shake — until fully dissolved.
  • Label with compound, concentration, and date immediately.
For laboratory research use only. This is not medical advice and not dosing guidance.

Research Peptide Storage & Handling: A Lab Reference

Lyophilized vs reconstituted storage, light and moisture, freeze-thaw cycles, and labeling — the practical lab basics.

Lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptides

  • Short-term (weeks): 2–8°C is generally fine.
  • Long-term (months+): −20°C, sealed, with desiccant.
  • Keep away from moisture — open vials only at room temperature to avoid condensation.

Reconstituted peptides

  • Store at 2–8°C.
  • Generally used within ~30 days of reconstitution.
  • Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles — aliquot if you need long storage.

General handling

  • Protect from light (amber or boxed storage).
  • Label everything: compound, concentration, date reconstituted.
  • Use clean technique — swab septums before each draw.
